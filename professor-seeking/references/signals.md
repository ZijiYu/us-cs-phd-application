# Recruiting signals and career-stage checks

## Where to look, in order of reliability

1. **The professor's own homepage or lab page**: "Prospective students", "Openings", "Join us", "For students". Note the term ("Fall 2027"), any instructions ("don't email me, apply and list me", a required subject-line code, a form), and when the page was last updated if shown.
2. **Department pages**: PhD admissions pages that list faculty recruiting this cycle, new-faculty announcements.
3. **The professor's posts**: X/Twitter and Bluesky (search `from:<handle> PhD students`, or `"<name>" recruiting PhD`), LinkedIn posts.
4. **Community posts**: 1point3acres (一亩三分地) recruiting threads, Zhihu, Xiaohongshu, mailing lists such as ml-news. Useful for signals, weaker as proof.
5. **Indirect**: a new hire (almost always recruiting), a recent large grant or new center, students graduating soon. These are hints only; don't report them as "recruiting".

## Search phrasings

- `"<professor name>" "prospective students"`
- `"<professor name>" recruiting PhD 2027`
- `"<school>" "looking for PhD students" 2027`
- `site:linkedin.com/posts "<school>" "PhD students" 2027`
- `site:x.com "<professor name>" PhD students`
- `一亩三分地 <school> <professor name> 招生` / `<school> 博士 招生 2027`

## LinkedIn

- Start with a school-level scan of public posts through web search. If nothing useful comes back, skip and continue; LinkedIn is a bonus, not a required step.
- Per-professor LinkedIn checks happen only for the shortlist, after scoring.
- LinkedIn profile pages need a login, so web fetching usually can't read them. Use the user's own logged-in browser only with their explicit permission: read-only, no connection requests, messages or reactions, and only a handful of pages.

## Recording a signal

Write each signal as: `signal type | exact quote (short) | URL | date seen | term covered`. For example:
`homepage | "I am looking for PhD students starting in Fall 2027" | https://... | 2026-11-05 | Fall 2027`.

If nothing is found, write `not publicly confirmed (checked homepage, X, dept page on <date>)`. That line is more useful later than a blank.

## Verifying career stage

- CV line such as "Assistant Professor, <School>, 2025–present".
- Department news: "welcomes N new faculty".
- LinkedIn experience section (with permission, as above).
- First paper with the new affiliation (DBLP or OpenAlex) as a fallback; say it is an estimate.

Flag "building a first cohort" when the start date is this or last academic year. New assistant professors often answer emails, can admit outside the main committee cycle, and are more open to applicants without a perfect profile, which matters most for safety and target cycles.
